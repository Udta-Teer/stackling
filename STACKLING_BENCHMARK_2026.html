STACKLING · COMPETITIVE BENCHMARK · 19 JULY 2026

We moved up. We are not world-class yet.

Stackling has become unusually strong at guided, visual system-design foundations. It now beats the established interview courses on interaction density and beats general learning apps on subject depth. It still loses the decisive endgame: diagnosing an individual learner, scheduling durable review, and evaluating a system designed from an empty canvas.

Current position: a credible category challenger with a distinctive product thesis—not yet the best product in the category. Stackling is strongest from “I barely understand servers” through guided architecture reasoning. Educative and DesignGurus remain stronger for interview simulation and independent end-to-end design; Brilliant and Duolingo remain much stronger as adaptive, polished learning systems.

What changed since the previous benchmark

The course is no longer mostly cards and quizzes

The measurable interaction layer now includes 205 architecture builds, 81 before/after system changes, 30 threshold-based simulations, 34 flow builders, and 44 transfer problems. All 44 units contain at least three new diagram-retrieval drills. This is the largest genuine improvement: learners repeatedly manipulate architecture rather than merely recognize terminology.

Transfer is now designed, not hoped for

Every unit has one graded problem in a different domain from its teaching example. Correct-answer positions are balanced and all 44 render and grade in a real browser at 360px. Stackling now tests whether a mechanism survives a changed surface story.

The first session now demonstrates value

The accepted prologue gives one explicit system, one action—add a load balancer—and one measured recovery. This closes the earlier activation failure where beginners met trivia and hidden assumptions before experiencing useful reasoning.

The improvements increased content scale faster than learning intelligence

Stackling now has 1,319 exercises, but it still gives essentially the same path to every learner. More items increase practice inventory; they do not create personalization, spaced retention, misconception diagnosis, or evidence that a learner can design independently.

Benchmark set

Brilliant — pedagogy benchmark

Visual active learning, custom feedback, learner modeling, targeted practice, fading scaffolds, and a curriculum-aware tutor. Brilliant explicitly says practice sets remove visual aids and hints to test independent ability. Official methodology

Duolingo — habit and mobile benchmark

Short sessions, personalized pacing, game loops, reminders, and a highly polished path. Its public product positioning centers game-like challenges and tailored learning. Official product page

Educative Grokking — breadth and mock benchmark

Currently advertises 204 lessons, 139 quizzes, 26 hours, 13+ case studies, and 8 embedded AI mock interviews with timed scenarios and rubrics. Official course

DesignGurus Grokking — interview-framework benchmark

Currently advertises 83 lessons, 237 quizzes, interactive diagrams, a repeatable interview framework, 15+ case studies, and optional expert mock interviews. Official course

Emerging visual tools — direct interaction benchmark

DesignSnake and Planmay already market free-position drag-and-drop architecture, real-time or AI feedback, and problem libraries. They are less proven than the incumbents, but they attack Stackling's intended differentiation directly. DesignSnake · Planmay

Where Stackling now stands

1

Best plausible position: guided interactive foundations

Stackling can credibly aim to be the best way for a basic coder to acquire system-design intuition on a phone. None of the large interview courses combines this beginner ramp with this much architecture manipulation.

2

Strong differentiator: causal system changes

The combination of “change architecture → observe measured consequence → explain mechanism” is more educational than static diagrams and more domain-specific than generic STEM interactions.

3

Not yet defensible: best system-design preparation

A learner can complete Stackling without ever starting from ambiguity, drawing a complete original design, defending it, handling interviewer pushback, or returning weeks later to prove retention. The leaders own those outcomes today.

The next three moves, in order

  1. Build one real Guided Design Studio and rubric. Start with URL Shortener. Empty canvas, requirements, estimates, APIs/data, architecture, failures, revision, and multiple valid answers. This closes the largest credibility gap.
  2. Add concept evidence and spaced review. Tag exercises by concept and reasoning skill; record attempts, hints, latency, and transfer; schedule delayed mixed retrieval. This converts a course inventory into a learning engine.
  3. Add fading and failure injection. Reuse the same design through worked example → partial diagram → palette → blank canvas, then inject a traffic spike, dependency failure, and changed consistency requirement.
Do not respond by adding another 500 questions. Stackling has enough inventory to compete. The next leap comes from measuring what the learner can do without support and whether they can still do it later.

Method and caveats

Stackling counts are computed from the current JSON source and verified prototype on 19 July 2026. Competitor claims are taken from current official product/course pages; they are vendor claims, not independent efficacy studies. Scores are product judgments anchored to observable features, not statistically validated learning outcomes.
